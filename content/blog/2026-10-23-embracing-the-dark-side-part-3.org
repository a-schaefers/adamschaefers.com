* So-called Machines of Loving Grace
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: grace
:END:

Dario Amodei, the CEO of Anthropic, wrote /Machines of Loving Grace/.[fn:molg]

The essay takes for granted that equality and democracy are the answer.
Equality isn't the same as justice. And the old test of a government was never
how many rule. It's whether they rule "with a view to the common
interest."[fn:pol3]

For all its talk of liberty and democracy, it imagines "a country of geniuses
in a datacenter," and a technocracy where a few men hold the tokens. Pope Leo
XIV, who took his name for "another industrial revolution,"[fn:leoname] put it
plainly in /Magnifica Humanitas/: "A more moral AI is not enough if that
morality is determined by a few."[fn:mh]

It's the marketing of a megalomaniac. A country of geniuses, run by a few men,
needs nobody. Aristotle said the man who needs nobody "must be either a beast
or a god."[fn:pol1]

The 20th century killed some 231 million people by human
decision,[fn:leitenberg] in two world wars, under communism, in genocides and
man-made famines. If Amodei's machines of loving grace come true as written,
even those sins may pale beside them.

* The crossroads
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: crossroads
:END:

So who gets to hold the tokens? The genie is out of the bottle, and AI is at a
crossroads. I only see two roads.

The first is the American road. Decentralize it as far as it'll go, and protect
it the way the 1st and 2nd Amendments protect speech and arms, so no ruling
class can corner it. The catch is energy. Frontier compute eats power, so the
least of us get only what the overlords allow. Make self-hosted AI efficient
and this road could win. It could be the best one.

Pope Leo XIV would disarm AI, and he means "freeing technology from monopolistic
control."[fn:mh110] That's what the 2nd Amendment was for, no monopoly on force.

I have my doubts.

The second is the Empire. Merge the corporations with the state. Pay every
citizen a dividend. Military service for all. A strong hand at the top. Call it
what it is, fascism, the dark side of Star Wars. Darth Vader is a pragmatist.
We join the Empire, the robots serve us, and we expand. The hippies like
Stallman lost.[fn:stallman] The Jedi are dying out and turning into myth.

Does the man at the top rule for everybody, or for himself? One is a king, the
other a tyrant. Aquinas grants this much: "The rule of one man is more useful
than the rule of many," and yet "just as the government of a king is the best,
so the government of a tyrant is the worst."[fn:deregno] The trouble is which
one you get.

And the middle of the road? A few labs renting us the tokens, a few agencies
looking over their shoulders, and nobody answering for any of it. Nobody is
happy, and everybody gets the short end. It's also where we're headed. Flying cars before GTA 6. A boring
dystopia where my phone snitches on me if it suspects a mental health risk.

disarm does not mean rejecting technology, but preventing it from dominating
humanity."
[fn:stallman] Richard Stallman, who founded the free software movement ([[https://stallman.org/][stallman.org]]).

* Raise the voters
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: vote
:END:

I'd have made anyone who wants to vote write a dissertation
defending their right to vote, and defend it in front of a panel. Fail, and you
lose the vote.

But a panel deciding who gets to vote is just the experts again, a few men
holding the tokens. It's the same thing I can't stand in Amodei's essay.

So I'll amend it. Keep the dissertation. Drop the panel. Teach every citizen
grammar, logic and rhetoric until every one of them could write it and defend
it.

Don't shrink the vote to fit the voters. Raise the voters to fit the vote.

* Power to the people
:PROPERTIES:
:CUSTOM_ID: power
:END:

The path forward is the American one.

Decentralize the tool. Defend it like speech and arms.

Push the tech companies to make it efficient enough to run anywhere. And keep
the weights open. A datacenter in every town running a closed model is just
renting closer to home.

And local leaders, put AI datacenters everywhere, as common as the public
library. That's how you actually decentralize power in the USA. It takes the
power out of a few private hands, and it ends the pay to code, pay to win
racket that OpenAI and Anthropic are running now.

Add hardware prices coming back down, productivity and wealth going up, and
consumer AI coming into its own, and this could be the power to the people
move.

It's the old answer to kings and tyrants, too, a mixed government where the
people hold a share.[fn:st105] And "the best political community is formed by
citizens of the middle class."[fn:pol4] A datacenter in every town, owned by
the town, is a middle class of compute.

Or as Chesterton said in /The Superstition of Divorce/:[fn:divorce]

#+BEGIN_QUOTE
Too much capitalism does not mean too many capitalists, but too few
capitalists.
#+END_QUOTE

Too much AI doesn't mean too many people running it. It means too few.

I've always been about power to the people. Power to the common man.

Doesn't the mean say to split the difference, and take the middle of the road?
No. The mean is "relative to us," not the halfway point, and one extreme can
sit nearer to it than the other.[fn:eth28] Owning the tool sits a lot nearer
than renting from the overlords. A compromise between the two is renting with
extra steps.

And if we can't decentralize it? Then we're renting either way, and the
question is from whom. The middle is a few landlords. The Empire is one.

I'd still take the Empire over the middle. Aquinas weighed one ruler against
several, and found that rule by several "deviates into tyranny not less but
perhaps more frequently than a monarchy."[fn:deregno6] Either one can go bad.
The middle goes bad more often, and nobody answers for it.

Belloc would say the middle doesn't hold anyway. It slides into servility with
nobody choosing it. In /The Servile State/:[fn:servile]

#+BEGIN_QUOTE
If we do not restore the Institution of Property we cannot escape restoring the
Institution of Slavery... there is no third course.
#+END_QUOTE

The Empire's dividend sits on the servile side of that fork too, and he has a
point. A dividend you can lose for disobedience is a leash, and once the robots
do the work, you can't even go on strike.

Choosing it doesn't make the king good. It lets you set terms. Aquinas says a
king's power "should be so tempered that he cannot easily fall into tyranny,"
and that a people who gave themselves a king can see him "deposed or have his
power restricted" if he turns tyrant. He means the people acting together, by
"public authority," not "the private presumption of a few," and he'd sooner
tolerate a milder tyranny for a while than risk worse.[fn:deregno7] Slide into
it, and there are no terms to hold anybody to.

So if it's the Empire, the terms come first, while our labor, our votes and the
2nd Amendment still count for something. A king who rules for everybody. A mixed government, with the
people holding a share. And a state that knows it isn't God. That last one is
the line between a king and the fascism I named at the crossroads.[fn:statolatry]
The day it asks to be worshiped, it's an idol like any other, and I'm out.

Look at those terms and you're most of the way back to the American road.
That's the point.

Aquinas goes to the root: "Sin must therefore be done away with in order that
the scourge of tyrants may cease." No arrangement of compute saves a people that
has lost its virtue. That's church and the trivium again.

Either way, the middle of the road is where you get run over.

Slop will ship.

Slop will get better.

Humans will still be needed. Just fewer of them.

So we'd better learn to share the wealth, and share it justly. Evil will try to
use all this to build a communist utopia. Don't let it.

We'll see wealth like the world has never seen, with a robot army at our
