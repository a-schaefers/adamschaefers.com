#+TITLE: I was wrong about AI
#+DESCRIPTION: Partially.

In March I made [[https://enchant.games/journal/2026-03-07-ai-llms-fall-of-the-software-engineer-rise-of-the-software-artisan/][a video]] telling you to "tell your AI-pushing boss to get wrecked."

I'm changing positions.

* What I got wrong

I said it was unlikely to be a 10x gain.

I said AI code is mid, because it's trained on the average of all code.

I said the guessing machine can't be counted on.

It still guesses, but with a compiler, tests and a human review behind it, the guesses are good enough to count on. They're past the average now.

* The tradeoff

Every system a programmer has ever built is a pile of tradeoffs. With AI we trade more for speed than ever. Understanding, beauty, the fun of building it the old-fashioned way, and the skill itself, if we stop practicing it.

* A pragmatic approach

From here forward I'm trying to take a pragmatic approach, similar to how Linus Torvalds uses it in the Linux kernel.

Check out @@html:<a href="/blog/2026-11-05-using-large-language-models-like-linus/">my other post about that</a>@@, on how I use AI without letting my brain turn to slop. Or trying to, anyway.
