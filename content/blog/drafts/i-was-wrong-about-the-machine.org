#+TITLE: I was wrong about the machine
#+DESCRIPTION: In March I told you not to lean on AI to code. It got better than I thought.

In March I made [[https://enchant.games/journal/2026-03-07-ai-llms-fall-of-the-software-engineer-rise-of-the-software-artisan/][a video]] telling you to "tell your AI-pushing boss to get wrecked."

When Aristotle broke with his own teacher, he [[https://classics.mit.edu/Aristotle/nicomachaen.1.i.html][called it a duty]], "for the sake of maintaining the truth even to destroy what touches us closely." What touches me closely is six essays and a video against leaning on AI to code, all with my name on them.

I'm changing positions.

* What I said

I said it was unlikely to be a 10x gain.

I said AI code is mid, because it's trained on the average of all code.

I said the guessing machine can't be counted on.

I kept expecting it to hit a wall.

And I'd have signed a petition to pause the whole thing.

* It got better than I thought

I built a whole platform by myself, [[https://merecatholicity.com/][Mere Catholicity]], a library of old books, an AI librarian that answers from them, and a forum. It took a week to get it standing, and a couple of months to build all of it.

[[https://github.com/merecatholicity/merecatholicity.com][The commit log]] kept the day. On the seventh morning the site was a book, some papers and a shelf of old texts. In under four hours, a comment box on seven of its pages grew into a forum with thirteen rooms. Before the next morning it had profiles, avatars and private messages.

By the end of that first week, I'd have bet the farm that software engineers were cooked.

It wasn't a one-off. My own Linux distro and an init system written in Emacs Lisp each took a week or two to stand up, on ground I already knew.

And it isn't magic. My own Vulkan driver for AMD cards got to the GPU in a week, and it's still stuck on its first triangle.

But it kept improving. People told me AI changes everything, and they had a point. It still guesses, but with a compiler, tests and a human review behind it, the guesses are good enough to count on for the work I've given it. And it's past the average now.

In /The fall of the software engineer/, the post with that video, I quoted a junior engineer at a big tech company who wrote that "Claude Code is able to do my job better than I can." I filed it under the problem, one more of the "very real frustrations of real software engineers" made to use AI.

I should have listened.

* The tradeoff

Nobody should know tradeoffs better than a programmer. Every system we've ever built is a pile of them.

We trade robustness for cost. Three regions and a hot standby, or one box and a backup you hope works.

We trade beautiful code for speed.

We trade owning for renting. Every managed service is understanding we pay somebody else to hold.

None of these are sins. The sin is pretending you aren't making a tradeoff.

I wrote that we trade understanding for speed, like that settled it. AI is the biggest tradeoff on the table, and I'd been treating it like a sin.

* What I do now

I use it, as a tool. Even Linus Torvalds does. [[https://www.tomshardware.com/software/linux/linus-torvalds-posts-his-first-llm-assisted-patch-to-the-linux-kernel-after-issuing-clear-pro-ai-directives-the-bossman-makes-use-of-ai-tooling-himself][His LLM-assisted patches]] each carry an "Assisted-by: LLM" line, right next to his "Signed-off-by." When Linus signs his patch, he owns it.

Most of us aren't Linus, and [[https://x.com/mitchellh/status/2100249348345057389][Mitchell Hashimoto]] has a bar the rest of us can reach. Somebody should be able to pull you up to a whiteboard and have you explain what you shipped, why X and not Y, where it fails, and what happens when someone acts maliciously.

If you can't, you don't own it.

I haven't always met that bar. When that last question got put to my own platform, it turned up a hole in the encryption on those private messages, one the types, the tests and the pipeline had all passed.

* What hasn't changed

I still don't think it can think.

It doesn't need to think to beat most of us at a lot of the job.

And the danger is still people who stop thinking, not a machine that starts. AI isn't going to become human. Humans are going to become NPCs.

I called using the machine the dark side. Fear was the dark side, and I was already there. What I'm embracing is a tool, used with a mind.

* What's next

I used Claude, under my direction, to research and draft it, and I criticize Claude's maker in it. Claude helped draft this post too.
